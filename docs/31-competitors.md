# Three other ways to learn Estonian, and where Kodukeel stands against them

Read on **2026-09-25**. Somebody deciding how to learn Estonian in Estonia will look at more than one
thing, and three of the things they are likely to find are Keelix, TereTere and Keeleklikk. This page
says what each of them offers, off their own pages, and puts Kodukeel's answer beside it with the
file or the route that delivers it. It is for whoever decides what to build next, and it exists so
that decision is made against what the others actually do rather than against a memory of them.

**Every claim about another service here carries the page it was read from and the date.** Nothing
was filled in from memory. Where a page could not be read, the row says so rather than guessing.
None of this appears in the app: the app names no other service, and nothing here is a claim about
their quality, only about what their own pages say they offer.

**The point is not to copy them.** A row marked BEHIND is a learner need somebody else meets and
Kodukeel does not. Where meeting it the way they do would break a rule this project holds (ADR-005,
no Estonian written by this app; ADR-018, no pronunciation score; `docs/22-real-life.md`, no
engagement tricks), the row says what was built instead, and why that version is the one a learner
is better served by.

## 1. What each one is, in their own words

**Keelix** (`https://www.keelix.ee/`) is exam preparation for the A2 and B1 state examinations.
Its front page offers "100 realistic mock tests", 25 for each of reading, writing, listening and
speaking across A2 and B1, "scored the way Harno scores it", with "model answers" for writing and
"AI feedback on speaking", a readiness certificate, and a free first set in every section with no
account. The rest is paid: 9.99 euros a week, 22.99 a month or 44.99 for three months, as listed
there on the day it was read. It also publishes long guides to the A2 and B1 examinations
(`/estonian-a2-exam`, `/estonian-b1-exam`), a guide to residence and citizenship
(`/residence-citizenship`), a guide and a free mock for the Constitution and Citizenship Act
examination (`/constitution-exam`), and a tips blog (`/tips`). Its readiness certificate page
(`/certificate`) says the certificate unlocks after a pass in every section and a sixty percent
average across three exams, and calls itself "a practice readiness check, not an official
certificate". The front page says ten exams for the same thing; the two pages disagree.

**TereTere** (`https://teretere.ee/`) promises B1 "in twelve focused weeks" with "short lessons and
realistic practice for every exam skill". The front page shows a day of "18 MINUTES" made of three
steps (learn, recall, listen) and a sample lesson a visitor can answer without an account. Its terms
(`/terms`) and refunds page (`/refunds`) put Premium at 9.99 euros a month or 26.99 for three months.
Its privacy notice (`/privacy`, updated 15 September 2026) says lesson progress, saved words and
practice results are kept in the browser's local storage and "not currently synchronised" to the
account, that course audio is generated by Microsoft Azure AI Speech, and that speaking can be
recorded, with an optional browser speech recognition that may send audio to the browser's provider.
Its terms say it "does not guarantee an exam result, qualification, or language level".

**Keeleklikk** (`https://www.keeleklikk.ee/`) is the state's free e-course, owned by the Ministry of
Education and Research and funded by the European Structural Funds, according to its about page
(`/en/info/about`). Three courses: Keeleklikk for 0 to A2, Keeletee for B1 and Keelelend for B2. The
first can be studied through English, Russian and Ukrainian, the second through English and Russian,
the third in Estonian only. Chapters "consist of interviews, animations, grammar videos, exercises and
tests", and in the first two courses "learners are supported by a teacher, to whom they can send
their written and spoken texts by email". The course map (`/en/A/coursemap`) lists sixteen chapters
for the first course and (`/en/B1/coursemap`) thirteen for the second. The help page (`/en/info/help`)
says videos can be slowed and subtitled, that the course bookmarks where somebody left off, and that
"once the exam is completed, you can view an overview and receive your diploma". A lesson list
(`/en/A/coursemap/list/1`) shows animated dialogues, grammar tutorials, and exercises called
"Practice speaking to the computer". The course's own dictionary (`/en/dictionary`) lists named
people with the chapters they appear in (Aksel in 4.1.9 and 5.1.2, Anna in 2.1.6), which is the
recurring cast the course is built around.

**And the state's own offer**, which all three sit beside. Harno's page on the examinations
(`https://harno.ee/en/examinations-tests-and-studies/examinations-tests-and-certificates/estonian-language-proficiency`,
read the same day) says the examination is free of charge, that a free consultation is held before
each one, that written samples by previous candidates are published with comments, and that public
e-tests are available in EIS. It also says a citizenship applicant needs the language examination at
B1 or above and the examination on the Constitution and the Citizenship Act
(`https://harno.ee/eesti-keele-tasemeeksamid`).

the learner; **EQUAL** means the need is met as well; **BEHIND** means somebody else meets a need
Kodukeel does not. The last column is what closed it, and it is updated as the work lands.

## 3. Where the BEHIND rows go

In the order a learner would feel them, biggest first.

1. **What the examination is.** An in-app guide sourced from Harno's own pages, with the free
   consultation, the published writing samples and the public e-tests named, since those are the best
   free preparation there is and none of the three points at all of them.
2. **Numbered papers, and one part at a time.** A fixed, deterministic set per level that a learner can
   return to, checked for repeats and for drifting easy at that scale, and each part sittable alone.
3. **Writing feedback without a model answer.** A model answer would be this app writing Estonian
   (ADR-005). What is built instead is the checklist an examiner marks against, attested sentences
   showing each required word in use, and a link to Harno's own marked samples.
4. **A record of study.** What was measured, what was sat and when, said plainly to be a record kept by
   this app and not a certificate.
5. **A promise of time, computed.** The plan the app already computes, offered before sign up, from the
   visitor's own answers, never a fixed number.
6. **Trying it first.** Something to answer on the landing page, built from attested sentences.
7. **The spoken part's opening conversation.** A structured interview task in the mock exam, with
   the questions described in English, marked by the learner.
8. **The interface in Russian and Ukrainian.** A translation mechanism and the screens a newcomer
   meets first, machine translated and marked unreviewed.
9. **A recurring cast.** People who come back across situations, carried in English in the scene
   specs; no new Estonian.
10. **A human teacher.** The mechanism, behind a setting, for a deployment that has one to offer.
11. **Recordings.** Not faked. A marked place for them.
12. **The Constitution and Citizenship Act examination.** Named in the guide with Harno's own
    materials. Not built: its question bank is Harno's, it is about civics rather than Estonian, and a
    mock of it written here would be a claim about Estonian law this project is not placed to make.
