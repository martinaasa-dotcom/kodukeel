# C1: who is who, and what each part owns

C1 is eight parts, told about the same family a year after B2 (`docs/reviews/b2-cast.md`), and
planned in `docs/43-lessons.md` §15. A name means one person for the whole course, A1 to C1. Read
this page before you write a line that names anybody, teaches a word or explains a pattern. Eight
parts are written side by side, so this page is also what keeps them from teaching the same thing
twice: the ledger fails on a word taught twice and on a pattern explained twice.

## Where B2 left them, and where C1 picks up

B2's last lesson (`b2-48-end`) is Tom at the B2 speaking exam with Kersti, in the autumn of the
family's second year. C1 opens three weeks later. **The first line of C1 is the certificate
arriving**: Tom passed B2. In the same week Liis posts in the friends' chat that the baby has come.
So the handover is one sentence a learner recognizes from where they were yesterday, and the first
thing C1 does is say well done, in Estonian, the way friends do.

Nothing B2 taught is taught again. Everything B2 explained (the quotative, the converb, the -ma
word's cases, nominalization, hedging, concession, the spoken register) is used freely from the
first line, and every C1 part recycles at least one B2 pattern in its reworks (the contract checks
it). The B2 exam Tom sat is the floor C1 stands on, and the C1 exam he sits in part 8 is the top of
the course.

## The parts

Lesson numbers are fixed so the parts can be written at once. A part's last lesson is its
`partEnd`, with a role-play and a live scene; every lesson id is `c1-NN-<slug>`. Part 8 has seven
lessons, so C1 has forty-nine, like B1: the C1 exam is a core lesson and the part ends on the
celebration after it.

| # | Part id | Lessons | Patterns it explains (once) | Its C1 units | Its text (`write`) | Its end scene | Its errand |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | `c1-isiksus` Being yourself | 1 to 6 | `jussive`, `stance` | `idioomid`, `nuansid` | a letter to a friend with two pieces of news, one happy and one sad (180) | `c1-nimepidu`: Mihkel's naming party, Tom's toast, a guest switches to English, a joke to understand | say a toast, a congratulation or a condolence in Estonian |

| 4 | `c1-kool` The school from the inside | 19 to 24 | `cohesion` | `lauseloome`, `diskursus` | a formal letter to the head of the school (200) | `c1-direktor`: the meeting with Piret and Toomas about Mark being left out | write to a school, a kindergarten or an office with a request, and send it |

A part may use, in any line or rework, every pattern a part above it explains, and from part 2 on at
least one rework recycles one C1 pattern from an earlier part; every part also recycles a B2 pattern
(the contract checks both).

## Who owns which words

A part teaches only words from its own areas below, plus the words its own life needs that no unit
names (each a request against Ekilex, like every lemma). Anything A1 to B2 taught it may simply use;
`npx tsx scripts/measure-b-levels.ts --through b2-48-end --spellings` prints every spelling already
taught. A word another part owns is said another way, or left out. The C1 units are in
`lib/collections/syllabus/c1.ts`, and they are a source rather than a quota (§15.1): teach what the
part's life needs, looking at the units first.

| Part | Its other words |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | birth and death (`rase`, `surema`, `matused`, `kaastunne`, `rahu`), feelings (`tänama`, `tänulik`, `armastus`, `üllatunud`, `hirmus`, `jube`, `suurepärane`, `kaunis`), the face and jokes (`nägu`, `loll`, `paha`, `pisike`), the exclamations (`kurat`, `pagan`, `jumal küll`), the stance words (`küllap`, `arvatavasti`, `nähtavasti`, `justkui`, `otsekui`) |
| 2 | media and news, science, rumor; `tõde`, `mingi`, `paistma` (`paistab, et`), `kaduma`, `täielik`, `kõva`, `arusaadav`, `kuulduma`; for the mystery: `tapma`, `süü`, `süüdi`, `süütu`, `relv` |
| 3 | buying a home (`kinnisvaramaakler`, `sissemakse`, `intress`, `laen`, `notar`, `leping`), damp and repairs; `laskma` (`lasta teha`), `ajama` (`asju ajama`), `endine`, `paks`, `pime`, `äri`; the formal connectives (`vastavalt`, `tulenevalt`, `arvestades`, `võrreldes`, `lähtudes`, `hoolimata`, `vaatamata`, `selmet`, `asemel`) |
| 4 | school life, homework, the grammar words schools use (`nimisõna`, `tegusõna`, `omadussõna`, `alus`, `öeldis`, `lause`, `koma`); `püüdma`, `aus`, `tegu`, `karm`, `näljane`; the signposts (`seejuures`, `ühtlasi`, `niisiis`, `järelikult`); the abbreviations (`jne`, `nt`) |
| 5 | leading, hiring, feedback, clients, boards; forms of address (`proua`, `härra`), `ideaalne`, `täiuslik`, `päästma`; emphasis particles not yet taught (check the ledger: `nimelt`, `alles`, `isegi`, `just`) |
| 6 | the city, elections (`volikogu`, `valimised`, `kandidaat`), consultation, volunteering; argument words (`seevastu`, `teisalt`, `kokkuvõttes`, `muuhulgas`, `õigupoolest`, `iseenesest`, `paraku`, `ehkki`); `sh`, `vrd` |
| 7 | the countryside, the bog, the islands, Setomaa, a village fair, a quiz, old songs, legends (`kuningas`, `püha`, `võimas`, `vägev`); `murre`; the dialect glossary (`DIALECT_WORDS`) is this part's to fill |
| 8 | books, ideas, ethics, a university course, the exam; collocations (`otsust langetama`, `küsimust tõstatama`, `rolli mängima`); near-synonyms |

The subtitle vocabulary of the 400 commonest words (`kolonel`, `kapten`, `kindral`, `isand`,
`preili`, `kutt`) is left for no part.

## The year

1. **Autumn.** Tom's B2 certificate. Mihkel is born; Heino dies. Jokes, toasts, feelings.
2. **Late autumn.** The morning show, the news, science on the radio, a rumor at work, the mystery
   evening.
3. **Winter.** The family buys a home and leaves Urmas's apartment and Helgi's building.
4. **Late winter.** Mark's school from the inside: homework, messages, being left out.
5. **Spring.** Kristi moves on and Tom leads the team.
6. **Late spring.** The city's plan for the street in their new district.
7. **Summer.** Setomaa, an island, the village fair.
8. **Autumn again.** The book club, Anna's course, the C1 exam, the party.
